## Lesson — sparse logarithmic sampling can alias Ramsey oscillations

- Snapshots `903`–`905` used 100-point logarithmic sweeps and produced
  nonphysical, uncertain, under-covered, or non-reproducible fits for q3/q4/q5.
- A 2 MHz detuning has a 500 ns period. A 600-point linear 16–60000 ns sweep
  (about 100 ns spacing) resolved that oscillation and converted all three fits
  into stable accepted results. Prefer enough linear-time samples to resolve
  the detuning period whenever sparse/log sampling leaves unresolved bands or
  contradictory repeated fits.

## Operator instruction — 2026-10-02 Asia/Taipei: detuning x window ~= 4 MHz*us

  fringes are easy to read: 1 us -> 4 MHz, 10 us -> 0.4 MHz (about four periods per

  4.0`, tolerance 0.25) and `policy.py`: an omitted detuning is derived from
  `max_wait_time_in_ns`, an inconsistent explicit one is refused.

- The 2026-09-30 runs (2 MHz over 60 us, 120 MHz*us) would now be refused; pick the

## 2026-10-04 — short-window recheck exposes aliased detuning (q9, q10)

The 64 us window (160 ns step, Nyquist ~3 MHz) withheld q9/q10 fits. A 4 us
window with 16 ns steps and Delta = 1 MHz showed fast fringes: q9 f+ 18.2 /
f- 16.1 MHz, q10 f+ 12.2 / f- 10.6 MHz (FFT of the first 0.8 us). The
half-difference equals Delta, so the fringe is a real 11-17 MHz drive detuning,
not noise; fit gave 16.4 / 13.2 MHz, T2* 156 / 99 ns. Committed as a frequency
correction. Lesson: when a short-T2* qubit's fit is withheld, recheck with a
window of a few us and a step well below 1/(2 x expected detuning) before
calling it unmeasurable. After such a large correction, 04/05 were calibrated
off-resonance and should be re-checked.
